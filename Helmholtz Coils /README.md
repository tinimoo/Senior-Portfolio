#Helmholtz Coil Introduction

I developed this model as part of the Summer Undergraduate Research Fellowship hosted by Belmont University the summer after my sophomore year. At this point in my undergraduate, career, I was still considering majoring in physics and was excited to work on a physics-related project. I was working in conjunction with two other students who were building an experimental chamber in which to incubate zebra fish while exposing them to electromagnetic radiation. I was responsible for developing a simulation of the device used to generate the radiation: a Helmholtz Coil. I chose to include the poster I created for this project because it represents both my first experience presenting research and my first experience with programming a mathematical model. This project demonstrates my interest and ability to contribute to fields that are not strictly mathematics. While physics is one of the most math-heavy subjects, this project required a good deal of non-mathematical knowledge of physics, particularly electromagnetic fields. This project was a practical introduction to the kind of interdisciplinary thinking that I find so interesting, and which draws me to the field of applied math. If I could do anything differently with this project, I would have approached it with more planning and researched. I jumped right into programming, and while my model was successful, I often found myself frustrated by stumbling blocks that could have been avoided with better planning.
