# Senior-Portfolio
High Dimensional Simplicity investigates the connection between three-dimensional tetrahedrons and certain properties of equilateral triangles. Specifically, whether the axis of symmetry of a tetrahedron could be realized as the line passing through the “centers” of a two-dimensional equilateral triangle. This project was my first exposure to research in mathematics as well as my first presentation at a conference outside of Belmont. This project kickstarted my interest in research and exposed me to almost entirely unfamiliar mathematical territory. I feel that what this project best demonstrates is my willingness to stick with a difficult project, even when the material is unfamiliar and progress is slow. This project ended up becoming much more difficult that we had anticipated, and I found myself forced to learn and work with more advanced topics than I was used to. While I found this frustrating, I also found myself excited by the challenge and the opportunity to learn more. The final product, a poster presentation at MAA MathFest, made me feel like the struggle had been worth it. If I could do anything differently, I would have more faith in myself to learn difficult concepts. I often felt overwhelmed by the material and at points feel that I was more afraid of doing something wrong than curious to better understand the result. This project helped me develop the tolerance for failure I have now, but it was difficult to work through at the time.
