# Senior-Portfolio Introduction

My first experience with research was in pure math within the field of projective geometry. I had never heard of the field before I attended a talk given by Dr. Adam Cartisano on its applications in art. I bombarded him with so many questions afterwards that we set up a meeting to talk in greater detail and he invited me onto one of his projects. While unexpectedly challenging, being exposed to such unfamiliar concepts was exciting to me and gave me a window into the usefulness of topics that I had previously only seen in the classroom. At the end of the project, getting to see the pieces of the problem finally falling into place was deeply satisfying to me, and fueled my passion for learning and research. 
Very broadly, I enjoy using mathematics as a tool to better understand real-world phenomena.  I am particularly interested in mathematical modeling and simulation, partial differential equations, and data analysis. Mathematical modeling and simulation appeal to me because of its applicability to many of the fields I am interested in, and it is the field which I am most interested in pursuing as a career. Two of the research projects included in this portfolio, Simulating the Magnetic Field of a Helmholtz Coil and Continuous Data Assimilation for Geophysical Flows, while both focusing on mathematical and computational modeling, addressed topics in the differing fields of electrodynamics and oceanography. The former also utilized partial differential, another field of interest to me. Partial differential equations are widely used in mathematical modeling and simulation but comprise their own body of research as well. I am fascinated by the ability of these equations to describe so many systems in wildly different fields, from riot modeling to quantum mechanics. Data analysis appeals to me for similar reasons. It can become very abstract and often involves high-level mathematical concepts but is increasingly becoming a vital tool in understanding complex systems, especially human systems. While these topics are especially appealing to me, they certainly do not represent the total of my mathematical interests, as evidenced by  the diversity of research in my portfolio. While I have discovered a love for applied math, I hope to continue to learn about many different fields and pursue interdisciplinary research.
