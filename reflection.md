#Reflection
If I’d presented this portfolio to myself as a freshman she’d probably be surprised that research was an option. Other than having fun and doing well in my classes and I didn’t have any concrete goals coming into my freshman year, and certainly wouldn’t have imagined that mathematical research would become so important to my career goals. The research I’ve done is quite varied, from projective geometry and combinatorial game theory to computational physics and oceanography. I chose to include these projects both because they are the most polished examples of my research but also because they show my widely varied interests and the skills I’ve acquired as a result. As an undergraduate, my goal as a mathematician has been to explore as many different topics as I can as deeply as I can. The variety in research in this portfolio demonstrates to me that I’ve been successful, not only in studying many interesting topics, but in contributing to them and growing both my own and the mathematical community’s knowledge. 

Looking back at four years of work at once I’ve noticed how my contributions to each project have increased with time. While working on my first project, High Dimensional Simplicity, I often felt that the material was so advanced that I could barely understand it, let alone contribute to it. I helped to prove one small lemma but mostly felt that I was just along for the ride. With my most recent project however, Continuous Data Assimilation for Geophysical Flow, while I did not develop any of the theory behind the project I still did most of the programming and experimentation myself with more limited help from my teammates. My increased ability to actively contribute to research shows the skills and independence I’ve developed as a mathematician. I’ve become more confident in my abilities, but I’ve also become more experienced and capable. Rather than feeling completely stuck when unsure of how to solve a problem, I’ve developed the ability to struggle more productively and to move forward during periods of uncertainty.
